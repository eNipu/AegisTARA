## Goal

Facts from the earlier slices (100a, 100b) to write down, do not change them:
- The vendor runs `scripts/offline-bundle.sh --manifest UPDATE.aegis --installer FILE [--installer FILE]... [--images IMAGES.tar.gz] --key KEYFILE --keys crates/aegis-license/keys/update.keys --out DIR`. It makes `aegistara-<release>-offline.tar` and `aegistara-<release>-offline.tar.sha256`. `--images` is made with `docker build -f deploy/Dockerfile -t aegistara-server:<release> .` then `docker save aegistara-server:<release> postgres:16 | gzip -n > images.tar.gz`. It is optional: a desktop-only (standalone) customer gets installers only.
- Inside the tar is one folder `aegistara-<release>-offline/` with `SHA256SUMS` (GNU `sha256sum` format, sorted by path), `SHA256SUMS.sig` (raw 64-byte Ed25519 signature over `SHA256SUMS`, made with the **update** key, the same key that signs update manifests), `update.aegis` (the signed update manifest), `installers/<files>`, and optionally `images.tar.gz`.
- The customer gets the vendor's update public key as a PEM file out of band (with the contract, or read aloud by fingerprint). The vendor makes it with `aegis-license public-pem --keys crates/aegis-license/keys/update.keys --key-id ID`. The PEM inside a delivery is never the trust anchor.
- Customer verification needs OpenSSL 3 or later (not LibreSSL): `openssl pkeyutl -verify -pubin -inkey aegistara-update.pem -rawin -in SHA256SUMS -sigfile SHA256SUMS.sig` prints `Signature Verified Successfully`; then `sha256sum -c --strict SHA256SUMS` (macOS: `shasum -a 256 -c --strict SHA256SUMS`) checks every file. The vendor's release CI also runs `aegis-license verify-bundle DIR --keys update.keys`, which further refuses unlisted files, symlinks, and installers whose hash is not in `update.aegis`.
- Why raw Ed25519 over plain text, not the `AEGIS-*` envelope: OpenSSL can check it, so a security team does not have to trust a vendor binary to check vendor binaries. Domain separation: the signed text must parse as sums lines (64 hex digits, two spaces, a path); manifest and license signatures cover messages starting with `aegis-update-v1\0` and `aegis-license-v1\0`, which never parse as sums lines, and the issuer refuses to sign anything that does not parse.
- Why the update key: it is already the one key allowed to ship code (ADR 0034); no new key to buy, store or rotate.
- Caps: `SHA256SUMS` at most 32 KiB, at most 64 files, paths at most 200 bytes and 2 levels deep.
- The outer tar is a wrapper only; its bytes are not deterministic, the signed contents are.
- `scripts/offline-bundle-test.sh` builds, verifies and tampers a bundle with a throwaway key; the CI `check` job runs it.
