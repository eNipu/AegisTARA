State what the chain does and does not prove: it shows a change made behind the server's back; it does not stop a database administrator from replacing the whole table together with its chain head unless the head is kept elsewhere. The project operation history is never trimmed; it lives as long as the project. Sessions: removed when they expire. Deleting a project deletes its operations, baselines and figures in one transaction. A longer legal keeping period: raise the retention or copy the table out (`Customer`); an archive export is `Planned` only if a requirement or ADR names it (ADR 0046 "Kept for later"), say so.

The customer runs the server and decides who uses it: the customer is the controller (GDPR) and the business operator handling personal information (APPI). State the limit honestly: the project operation history and the event log are hash-chained, so a user name inside them cannot be changed or removed one entry at a time without breaking the chain. What a customer can do: use company login so the stored identifier is the identity provider's subject and not a personal name (confirm what the subject looks like in `crates/aegis-server/src/auth.rs` and `auth_layer.rs` before you promise this), or use role or pseudonymous user names for password login, and set the event retention to its policy.

   - `## 5.5 GDPR mapping`: a table: Article 5 (minimisation, storage limitation), 25 (by design and by default), 30 (records of processing: the inventory in 5.2 is the input), 32 (security: refer to chapters 2, 3 and 4), 15 to 17 (5.4), 33 and 34 (breach: the event log and the vendor's vulnerability handling in `SECURITY.md`), 44 (transfers: the product sends no data outside the customer's deployment), 28 (processor: the vendor is not one unless support data is handed over). Give article numbers only if you are certain of them; a duty named without a number is better than a wrong number. Say in build.md which numbers you gave and why you are sure.
